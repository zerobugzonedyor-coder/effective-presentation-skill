# Ten Simple Rules (PLOS + Duarte synthesis)

Adapted from Kristen M. Naegle, PLOS Computational Biology (2021) and Nancy Duarte density guidance.

## Scope Rules
1. One idea per slide — split complex background or results.
2. ≤ 1 minute speaking time per slide.
3. Title = the message (action/assertion title).
4. Include only what you will explicitly discuss.
5. Use the heading as the abstract of the slide.

## Design Rules
6. Prefer graphics over text-only slides.
7. Design to avoid cognitive overload (dual-channel theory: do not force simultaneous reading + listening).
8. High contrast, large fonts, consistent alignment, generous whitespace.

## Delivery Rules
9. Practice with the actual slides; refine based on timing and flow.
10. Prepare for failure modes (backup PDF, no reliance on complex animations).

## Density Spectrum (Duarte)
- **Delivered live**: sparse, conceptual, memorable. Slides support the speaker.
- **Read-then-discuss** (most meetings): enough density to be understood before discussion, but still focused.
- **Read-alone** (reports, case studies): denser, self-contained, but never walls of text.

Never apply “always sparse” or “always dense” blindly.
